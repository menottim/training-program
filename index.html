Achilles Protocol click to expand

Achilles Tendinopathy Protocol

Both sides. Heavy Slow Resistance (HSR) calf raises, 3 loaded sessions per week. This week's plan names the days.

Protocol: 3 loaded sessions per week

3 sets per session. Tempo 3s up, 3s down. Do both variants: seated (bent knee, soleus) and standing (straight knee, gastroc). Bodyweight raises do not count toward the 3.

Progressive 12-week load

WeeksLoadReps per set
115RM15
2-312RM12
4-510RM10
6-88RM8
9-126RM6

Heavy must be heavy. Each set is a true rep max: the last rep is the last one you can do at full tempo. If every rep feels easy, add weight. Never add reps.

What to do about pain

  • 0 to 3/10 during loading: fine. Keep going.
  • 4 to 5/10: hold the load. Do not add weight.
  • Above 5/10: stop the exercise. Cut the load 25% next session.
  • 24-hour rule: pain must be back to baseline by the next day. If not, the load was too high. Tell me.
  • Morning stiffness over 30 minutes: tell me.
  • Always step down from box jumps. Never rebound.
  • HSR stays at full load even during a deload. Tendons adapt on a months-long clock, not a weekly one.

Worth trying

  • ESWT if HSR plateaus at 12 weeks for a midportion (not insertional) tendon. Keep loading during it.

Not recommended

  • PRP injection. No better than a saline shot when added to loading.
  • GTN patches. Weak evidence, and a real headache side effect.

When the Plan Changes

Deloads are reactive, not scheduled. Tell me when something feels different, and I adjust the week.

What triggers a deload

Two or more of these:

  • Working sets feel 1 to 2 RPE harder than last week at the same weight.
  • Performance drops more than 5% at the same effort.
  • Achilles morning stiffness lasts over 30 minutes.
  • Games feel clearly worse.

What a deload actually changes

  • Volume drops roughly in half (4×5 becomes 2×5, 3×8 becomes 2×8).
  • Weight stays about the same. Dropping it costs strength and adds no recovery.
  • Achilles HSR work stays at full load.
  • Games run as normal. A deload only touches lifting.
The masters-athlete rule Recovery is your limiting factor, not training capacity. When in doubt, do less. You can always add. You can't un-injure.
