# Blood Donation and Athletic Recovery (Power Red / Double Red Cell)

## Scope

This file covers three topics. First, the physiological and performance consequences of a whole blood donation and of a Double Red Cell donation, which is also called Power Red or DRCA. Second, the recovery timeline for oxygen-carrying capacity and for endurance performance. Third, the role of iron supplementation in that recovery.

The file is calibrated for Menotti. He donates Power Red 2 to 3 times per year, and he plays in-season recreational basketball and hockey.

[supplements.md](supplements.md) develops the iron-status and supplementation detail. This file covers the performance side and the training response.

This file does not cover donation eligibility criteria. It does not cover the clinical management of anemia.

## Athlete context

Menotti donates Double Reds, which is Power Red. That procedure removes __two units of red cells__ in one session. A whole blood donation removes one unit.

[supplements.md](supplements.md) records the details. Each Power Red removes approximately 470 mg of iron, against 220 to 250 mg for whole blood. Full replacement of the iron stores takes 24 to 30 weeks. The American Red Cross requires a 112-day wait between Power Red donations, against 56 days for whole blood.

He already takes 65 mg of elemental iron plus 125 mg of vitamin C each day. That is appropriate, and he must continue it through the interval between donations.

One caveat on the evidence below. The performance literature is almost entirely on a __single unit of whole blood__. Power Red removes approximately double the red-cell mass. The magnitudes and the timelines here are therefore extrapolated upward, and this file flags that where it matters.

## Mechanism

### The acute cost runs on two separate clocks

Removing blood lowers performance through two mechanisms. Each one recovers at a different rate.

1. __Blood-volume loss, the fast clock.__ Less circulating volume lowers stroke volume and cardiac filling, which drops VO2max immediately. Plasma volume re-expands inside approximately 3 days, through fluid shifts and fluid retention. This component therefore largely resolves by day 3. Hill 2013 showed this clearly. A plasma-only donation removes volume but keeps the red cells. It cut time to exhaustion temporarily. It did __not__ reduce VO2max once measured beyond the acute window, because the donor kept the red cells.

2. __Red-cell-mass deficit, the slow clock.__ Fewer red cells means lower total hemoglobin and less oxygen-carrying capacity. This is the limiting factor. It recovers only as fast as erythropoiesis rebuilds the red cells. Erythropoietin, iron availability and marrow capacity govern that rate. For one whole-blood unit it takes approximately 3 to 4 weeks (Meurrens 2016). For Power Red, which removes approximately 2 units, it takes longer. The 112-day Red Cross deferral is the regulatory proxy for that longer recovery.

Hemoglobin and aerobic capacity relate approximately proportionally. Across the range of 11 to 16 g/dL, each drop of approximately 0.3 g/dL in hemoglobin is associated with a drop of approximately 1% in VO2max. The size of the aerobic cost therefore follows the size of the red-cell loss. That is why Power Red costs more than whole blood.

### Why power and strength survive while endurance suffers

The deficit is specifically in __oxygen delivery__.

Maximal strength and short explosive efforts run primarily on the anaerobic ATP-PCr system and the glycolytic system. Those include a jump, a sprint and a single hard shift. Neither system depends on oxygen transport in the moment.

Endurance, repeated high-intensity efforts, and recovery between efforts all depend on aerobic oxygen delivery. Those suffer most.

Hill 2013 found the anaerobic responses far less affected than the aerobic ones. This is why an athlete reports that his wind is down but his legs feel fine.

### What this means for intermittent court and ice sports

Basketball and hockey are intermittent. Anaerobic skill bursts alternate with lower-intensity periods or with bench time.

The pure-endurance deficits measured on a cycle-ergometer test to exhaustion, which produce the VO2max figures of approximately 10 to 15%, overstate the effect on a 20 to 40 minute intermittent session.

The real-world signal is different. Expect an elevated heart rate and a higher RPE at a given effort, faster fatigue late in a session, and slower recovery between bouts or shifts. Do not expect a loss of skill or of power.

## Iron: what it does and what it does not do

This is the part people misunderstand most often, and the nuance matters for a repeat donor.

- __Iron is the rate-limiting substrate for hemoglobin synthesis.__ Supplementation reliably and substantially speeds up the return of hemoglobin and of the iron stores. Kiss 2015, the REDS-III trial, cut the return to baseline from 23 weeks to 5 weeks in slow recoverers. This is Strong evidence, and it is the clinical basis for the Red Cross recommendation to supplement after a Power Red.
- __After one donation, iron does not speed up performance recovery.__ Time-trial performance returns inside approximately 1 week, with or without iron (2025 JSCR). In an iron-replete person, the short-term performance rebound follows plasma-volume restoration and early erythropoiesis. It does not follow iron availability.
- __For a repeat donor, iron does protect performance over time.__ Across repeated donations, supplementation limits the cumulative endurance decline (2020 RCT, PMID 32931412). Menotti donates 2 to 3 Power Reds per year. Each one drains approximately 470 mg of iron, and store replacement takes 24 to 30 weeks. That is exactly the repeat-donor case where iron prevents a slow erosion of performance. His daily iron therefore does real work, both hematologically and for performance continuity. It will still not speed up the recovery of his legs after any one donation.

The conclusion: iron is necessary maintenance for him. It is not an acute recovery accelerant. Do not expect it to shorten the wind deficit after a given Power Red.

## Practical Application (calibrated for Menotti)

1. __Continue the daily iron, and optimize its absorption.__ Keep 65 mg of elemental iron plus 125 mg of vitamin C. Take it away from coffee, tea, dairy and calcium, because all four inhibit absorption. The vitamin C pairing already approximately doubles non-heme uptake. Add dietary heme iron from red meat, poultry and fish, which absorbs far better than plant iron. See [supplements.md](supplements.md) for the full dosing rationale, and for the caution about high-dose antioxidants around a workout.

2. __Train with the deficit for approximately 4 to 6 weeks after a Power Red. Do not fight it.__ Auto-regulate the conditioning by RPE and by feel, not by pace or output. A given effort will read 1 to 2 RPE points high. That is the donation, not detraining. This window suits a power-realization block, which is Phase 3, because power work depends least on oxygen. Lengthen the rest between power sets, so each effort stays fresh. Quality matters more than density here. Keep playing games, because they are the highest priority, and auto-regulate inside them: shorten a shift or a possession, and take full recovery when the wind drops.

3. __Support the rebuild.__ Hold the protein floor at 160 to 220 g per day, because erythropoiesis needs protein for globin synthesis. Hold adequate total calories. Stay well hydrated, to support plasma re-expansion in the first approximately 3 days. Do not overdrink. See [sodium-hydration.md](sodium-hydration.md): excess water dilutes serum sodium, and it does not flush anything. Protect 7.5 to 9 hours of sleep. See [recovery-sleep.md](recovery-sleep.md).

4. __Watch for donation-specific red flags in the first 24 to 72 hours.__ These differ from normal training fatigue: lightheadedness; dizziness on a hard stop; unusual breathlessness; or a heart rate that will not settle between efforts. Any of these means reduce the intensity or the volume. Do not train through them.

5. __Monitor ferritin.__ At the next donation, or at the annual physical, request a serum ferritin test. The target is 30 to 200 ng/mL, per [supplements.md](supplements.md). Many Vitalant and Red Cross centers run ferritin at donation. If recovery feels slow well past the expected window, ferritin shows whether iron status is the cause.

6. __Schedule a donation around the training intent.__ A Power Red costs least before a power or strength block, which depends little on aerobic capacity. It costs most before a conditioning-heavy block, or before a stretch of important games. If a future donation is discretionary, place it in a phase with low aerobic demand.

## Unresolved Questions

- __No study measures performance recovery after a Power Red donation specifically.__ Every performance timeline above comes from a single unit of whole blood. The estimate of 4 to 6 weeks or more for Power Red is extrapolated from the approximately 2x red-cell loss, plus the 112-day regulatory deferral. No one has established the precise VO2max recovery curve for Power Red. The tier is Emerging.
- __Limited trials support the link between iron and performance recovery in a repeat donor.__ The evidence is one repeated-donation RCT plus small single-donation trials. The direction of the effect is consistent. No one has characterized the effect size, or the optimal dose and timing, for a donor who gives Power Red 2 to 3 times per year. The tier is Moderate.
- __There is no data specific to an intermittent sport.__ The mapping from a cycle-ergometer VO2max deficit to basketball or hockey performance is mechanistic reasoning. No one has measured it.
